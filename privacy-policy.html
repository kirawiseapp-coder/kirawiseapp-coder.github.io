KiraWise Privacy Policy

Effective date: 21 August 2026 · Last updated: 1 November 2026

KiraWise does not collect your data. Everything you enter stays on your phone unless you pass it on yourself.

This version of the app ships without the Android internet permission, so it cannot connect to the internet. What you enter leaves the phone only when you pass it on yourself: for example, a backup you hand to another app through Android’s share sheet, or a bill split’s summary you copy and paste. Should a later version add an optional account for backing up your data, this policy will be updated and published before that version is released.

What KiraWise stores

Everything you type into KiraWise is kept inside the app’s own private storage on your device, in a database file and, for pictures, image files:

  • transactions, categories, and notes
  • income sources and fixed monthly expenses
  • payslips you enter for a salary: gross pay, EPF, SOCSO, EIS and other deductions, by month
  • savings goals and progress
  • monthly spending limits for categories, money you plan to set aside each month and what you actually moved, and your one-tap quick-spend buttons
  • accounts you create, including their names, colours and icons
  • bill splits: the items on the receipt, each person’s name, how much each of them owes, whether they have paid you back, and which of your accounts the money went into; and the names of the people you last split a bill with, kept to offer them for your next split
  • the ways you give friends to pay you back: a bank account number, a DuitNow ID (which may be a phone number or an IC number), an e-wallet number or a payment link, the name the account is under, and a picture of a payment QR code
  • your settings, such as theme and hourly rate

Android keeps this storage private to the app. Other apps cannot read it.

Copying a bill split’s summary puts on the phone’s clipboard a line for each person with their share, labelled with the items they had (or with their name, if they had none or the bill is split evenly), and the bill’s total; and, if you saved a way of being paid back other than a QR code, that one (the only one, or the one you pick): its name and number and the name on the account, or the link. KiraWise sends it nowhere; the phone, or a keyboard app, may keep it in a clipboard history (and some phones sync it to your other devices).

Photos

If you choose a photo for an account, KiraWise opens the phone’s own picker. You pick one image; the system hands only that image to the app. KiraWise never receives access to the rest of your photo library, which is why it does not ask for a photos permission.

The image you pick is cropped, resized to 512×512 pixels, and saved inside the app’s private storage. It is not uploaded anywhere.

A payment QR code you add under Settings → Getting paid back comes through the same picker. It is cropped, resized to 1024×1024 pixels so a friend’s phone can still scan it off your screen, read on your phone to check that it holds a code, and saved inside the app’s private storage. It is not uploaded anywhere either.

To crop either one, the plugin KiraWise uses to open the picker copies the image you chose into the app’s cache, and usually makes a smaller copy of that too. They are deleted when the crop screen closes, whether or not you keep the picture. If KiraWise is closed before then, they are deleted the next time you choose a picture or at a later start.

Backups you make yourself

Settings → Backup & restore makes one kind of file, only when you ask: a backup of all your data. It is handed to Android’s share sheet, where you choose what happens to it next.

KiraWise does not send this file anywhere. It has no internet permission and no ability to. Once you pick an app in the share sheet, the file is in that app’s hands and covered by that app’s privacy policy, not this one — so if you send a backup to a cloud drive or a chat app, please treat it as you would any other document containing your finances.

A file you export is a copy of your records as they were: deleting a record afterwards does not change it. KiraWise makes the file in its private cache and deletes it once the share sheet is done with it, or, if Android closes KiraWise first, the next time KiraWise starts. The share sheet is handed a copy of its own, which stays in the app’s private cache until the next time you share a file from KiraWise, or until the cache is cleared: by Android, or by you under Settings → Apps → KiraWise → Storage → Clear cache, which leaves your data as it is.

Restoring reads a file you pick and replaces what is currently in the app with its contents. To read it, the plugin KiraWise uses to open Android’s file picker copies the file into the app’s cache; KiraWise deletes that copy once it has read it, whether or not it could be restored.

What KiraWise does not do

  • No account, no sign-up, no login
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage tracking
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers
  • No location access
  • No contacts, calendar, microphone, or camera access
  • No selling or sharing of data, because no data is collected

Third-party components

KiraWise is built with open-source software libraries that run entirely on your device: a local database engine, a chart drawing library, date and number formatting, a QR code reader, the plugins that open the phone’s own photo and file pickers and hand a file to Android’s share sheet, and small plugins for the home-screen shortcuts, the app’s version number and its own folders. None of them contact a server (without the internet permission, none of them could), and none of them collect information about you.

Children

KiraWise is a general-audience budgeting tool. It is not directed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Because KiraWise keeps your data only on your device, you control it completely:

  • Delete individual records inside the app at any time.
  • Delete everything by going to Android Settings → Apps → KiraWise → Storage → Clear data (Clear storage on some phones), or simply by uninstalling KiraWise.

When you delete a record, KiraWise deletes it from its database. Some of what it said can still be elsewhere: in any backup you exported before, and the share sheet’s copy of the last file shared (see Backups you make yourself); in a bill split’s summary you copied, on the clipboard, in a clipboard history the phone or a keyboard app keeps (or on another device it synced to), or wherever you pasted it; and, for a bill split you delete, its people’s names, if they are among the names kept for your next split.

Test builds before version 1.0.0 (47) did not delete these cache copies: the picker’s copies of a chosen picture, the file made for the share sheet, and the copy of a file picked to restore. 1.0.0 (47) deletes the picker’s copies and the files made for the share sheet the first time it starts. The copy of a file picked to restore stays in the app’s private cache until Android clears it, or until you clear it yourself under Settings → Apps → KiraWise → Storage → Clear cache, which leaves your data as it is.

Those test builds could also export a spreadsheet of your transactions. One you exported is a copy of your records as they were: deleting a record does not change it.

Uninstalling removes the database, any account photos and any QR code pictures with it, and the app’s cache with whatever copy is left there. There is no server-side copy for us to delete, because there is no server.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of KiraWise adds a feature that sends data off your device — for example optional cloud backup — this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the app will ask for your consent before any data leaves your phone.
